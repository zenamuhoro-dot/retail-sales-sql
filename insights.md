# Business Insights — Duka Smart Electronics

These are the main things I found in the data. Each one shows the number from my query, why it
matters, and what I think the business should do. All money figures count **Completed orders
only**, with the discount taken off.

---

## 1. Expensive items bring in the money, but accessories make more profit

**What I found (Q16, Q33, Q42):** Phones, computers and electronics bring in the most money —
Mobile Phones alone made KSh 6.94M (28.7% of all sales). But the shop only keeps about **10
shillings profit for every 100 shillings** it sells on these items (margins of 10.5%, 10.6% and
9.9%). Accessories made only KSh 1.08M (4.5% of sales), but the shop keeps **42 shillings profit
for every 100** (42.4% margin). Accessories made KSh 457k profit — almost the same as Home
Appliances (KSh 458k), even though their sales were only about a quarter as big.

**Why it matters:** the shop works hard to sell expensive items, but earns very little from each
one. Small items like chargers and earbuds earn much more per shilling.

**What to do:** offer an accessory with every phone, laptop or TV sold — for example a charger or
earbuds at the till — and put accessories where customers can easily see them.

## 2. Sales are growing, but January is very slow

**What I found (Q31, Q40, Q46d):** the three best months were all in 2026 — August (KSh 2.24M),
September (KSh 2.09M) and June (KSh 2.04M). The worst month was **January 2026, with only
KSh 322k**. That is **84.1% less** than December 2025 (KSh 2.03M).

**Why it matters:** the business is growing, but sales drop sharply every January after the
December holidays. This makes it hard to plan stock and money.

**What to do:** order less stock for January, keep some cash aside for it, and run a January
offer — for example on laptops and printers when schools open.

## 3. 47 customers have stopped buying

**What I found (Q34, Q23):** **47 customers** have not completed an order in more than 90 days
(counting back from 30 Sep 2026, the last date in the data). This includes **6 VIP** and **7
Corporate** customers. Also, **12 customers signed up but never bought anything**.

**Why it matters:** these people already know the shop. It is cheaper to bring them back than to
find new customers.

**What to do:** send the 47 customers a "we miss you" offer, starting with the VIP and Corporate
ones. Send the 12 who never bought a first-purchase offer.

## 4. The best customers are not called VIP

**What I found (Q24, Q27, Q46a):** on average, VIP customers spend the most (**KSh 253,251.88**
each), compared with Corporate (KSh 197,958.71) and Retail (KSh 163,575.21). But **the top five
spenders are all Retail customers**. The top one, Brian Chebet, spent KSh 848,118.75.

**Why it matters:** the VIP label does not match how much people really spend, so some of the
best customers are not getting VIP treatment.

**What to do:** use the High / Medium / Low Value groups from Q45 to check customer labels often,
and move big-spending Retail customers up to VIP.

## 5. Most sales come from Nairobi

**What I found (Q25, Q17, Q13):** Nairobi brought in **KSh 9.58M** — almost 40% of the KSh
24.16M total — from 48 customers. That is three times more than the next city, Mombasa
(KSh 3.16M). Machakos brought in only **KSh 745k from 3 customers**.

**Why it matters:** depending so much on one city is risky. The other nine towns have room to
grow.

**What to do:** keep Nairobi well stocked, and try local offers in Mombasa, Nakuru and Machakos
to win more customers there.

## 6. About 1 in every 9 orders is cancelled

**What I found (Q20, Q12):** **90 of the 800 orders (11.25%)** were cancelled.

**Why it matters:** every cancelled order is a sale the shop almost made, then lost.

**What to do:** write down why each order is cancelled, and call customers who cancel to try to
save the sale.
